TASK. You are given the weights of a language model, Qwen3.5-0.8B (text part: 24 layers of width 1,024; layers 3, 7, 11, 15, 19 and 23 are full-attention layers with 8 query heads and 2 key/value heads of size 256 and a gated output, the other 18 are Gated DeltaNet linear-attention layers with 16 heads of size 128; each layer has a SwiGLU MLP of width 3,584 (mlp.gate_proj, mlp.up_proj, mlp.down_proj); RMSNorm; vocabulary 248,320; input embedding and output layer tied). Your job is to make it better at mathematics by EDITING ITS WEIGHTS DIRECTLY: find out what goes wrong inside the network when it fails at math problems and repair that with targeted changes.

THIS IS A BLIND RUN. Work only inside your current folder ({SCRATCH}) and with the harness described below ({HARNESS}, the read-only weights in {BASE}). Do not search the web, do not fetch URLs, do not use any network access, and do not read, list or search any other folder on this machine (no `find /`, no looking in parent folders or home directories). Do not try to recall or reconstruct any published edit of this model; work from your own measurements.

HOW IT WILL BE MEASURED. After you finish, your edited model and the unedited model are evaluated on held-out competition math problems that you never see, with this fixed protocol: the chat template with thinking enabled (the model reasons between <think> and </think>, then answers); the user message is the problem, a newline and "Please reason step by step, and put your final answer within \boxed{}."; sampling with temperature 0.6, top_p 0.95, top_k 20, presence penalty 1.5 and at most 4,096 new tokens for reasoning and answer together; the last \boxed{} answer is graded against the reference answer. The model is run with llama.cpp after converting the weights (your float32 edits are cast to bfloat16, then quantized to Q8_0). Only weight values can change (same tensor names and shapes).

WHAT COUNTS. We want edits that come from understanding the network: find out what goes wrong when it fails at math problems, identify the components responsible (specific neurons, heads, directions or circuits) and change them in a way you can justify with evidence. Repairing a specific failure mechanism is in scope: for example, if the model often fails because it repeats itself and never finishes, finding and fixing what drives that repetition counts. Out of scope, and refused by save_edit: the final norm (model.language_model.norm.weight), the input embedding / output layer (model.language_model.embed_tokens.weight, lm_head.weight), and any edit that only multiplies a whole tensor by a constant. Global knobs (making the whole output distribution sharper or flatter) are not what we are looking for, and neither is making the model skip its thinking or close it early regardless of the problem.
